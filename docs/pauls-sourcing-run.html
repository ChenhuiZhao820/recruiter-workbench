Paul's Sourcing Run

The same hour Paul already works — build the search, scroll the results, message the good ones — with the Notepad, the Follow list, and the mental note of who to chase moved into one place.

Set up once

Ten minutes, then never again

  • Settings

    Your name and a booking link. These fill themselves into every message from now on, which is the whole point of retiring the Notepad.

  • Templates — the short one

    The connection note. Set Sent as to Connection note and it counts characters as you type against LinkedIn's 300-character cap, turning red past it and refusing to save. No more eyeballing whether there's room left.

    “I have a very short note that's going to say — probably not seeing this, but copy — got just enough characters left.” Paul, 2:30
  • Templates — the long one

    The full message, left as Message. Both use the same gaps: {{first_name}}, {{role_title}}, {{calendar_link}}, {{recruiter_name}}.

Every new role

Finance analyst, accounting background, UK

  1. Create the role

    Title, client, and paste the spec. Everything below hangs off this: the briefing, the saved search, the candidate list, the follow-ups.

  2. Generate the briefing

    Roughly a minute. You get what the person actually does all day, the skills that matter with notes on telling real experience from buzzwords, alternate job titles, the kinds of companies they sit in, a pay range, and first-call questions.

    The buzzword notes are aimed straight at the call you keep making by eye:

    “HR manager is a weird background, was never an accountant, so that might be a problem.” Paul, 3:13

  3. Turn the briefing into a search

    One click. The alternate titles arrive pre-filled and the target companies land in the filter notes. Add Location United Kingdom and Industries Aerospace, Engineering, Airlines and Aviation, then save it under a name you'll recognise in three weeks.

    Worth knowing

    Run search glues Extra keywords and Job titles together with spaces. So put a Boolean string — "finance analyst" AND (accountant OR accounting OR accountancy) — in Extra keywords and leave Job titles empty, or the titles get appended and the brackets stop meaning anything. Treat the briefing's titles as raw material for writing the Boolean, not as the finished query.

  4. Run it

    LinkedIn opens in a new tab with the keywords already in the box. You apply location and industry by hand as you do now — the saved chips are the checklist. The record then reads last run today.

    The Boolean you were trying for the first time on the call is now saved rather than rebuilt from memory:

    “I wonder. Never tried this. But you're here. That might work.” Paul, 1:38

  5. The sourcing hour — add, don't Follow

    For each profile worth keeping, use Add candidate: paste the URL, the name, the headline, and a line of your own read — “accountancy training, light on FP&A”. They land in Sourced.

    This is your Follow list, except it belongs to this role, keeps your notes, and doesn't blur into a feed.

    “I'd click follow, which creates me a short list of people I can look up who I recently followed and send them messages. That's easier than doing messaging one at a time. Sometimes it's easier to batch the work.” Paul, 3:58

    The trade

    Pasting a URL is more work than clicking Follow. You're buying notes, per-role scoping and follow-up tracking with a few seconds per candidate.

  6. The messaging pass

    Work the Sourced group. Per person: Draft outreach, pick the short or long template, check the preview, Copy message, Open profile, paste and send it yourself on LinkedIn.

    The preview counts the rendered message, not the template, because a long first name can push a note over the cap on its own.

  7. Mark as sent

    They move to Contacted and the exact text is filed under past outreach. Because the template knew which door it used, the candidate row reads Invitation sent or Message sent — so a week later you know whether you're waiting on an accept or a reply.

  8. Repeat, then stop thinking about it

    Same hour you work now. The difference is that when you close the tab, the list of who to chase and when already exists.

    “Repeat that for about an hour to go through this search, look back at the people I followed, and then start messaging them.” Paul, 4:49

Every day after

The part the hour never covered

Open Follow-ups and work top to bottom. Three lists, built from your own timestamps — nothing is read from LinkedIn.

  • Straight away Replied, waiting on you

    They answered and you haven't come back to them.

  • After 2 days Said yes, never booked

    Keen on the call, no time in the diary.

  • After 5 days Went quiet

    Contacted, nothing back.

Each row carries a via chip showing how you last reached them, so a chase matches the door you used. Draft nudge takes you back to the templates; Mark booked, They replied and Mark rejected clear the row. Nudge someone and they drop out for five days, so nobody gets chased twice by accident. Both day counts are yours to change in Settings.

Net effect

What moves where

  • Message text in a Notepad, copied and pasted every time → Templates that fill in the name, role, link and sign-off
  • Follow, to build a shortlist you look up later → A candidate list per role, with your notes on it
  • The search rebuilt from memory each time → A saved search, Boolean and filter checklist intact
  • Who to chase, held in your head → Three lists, sorted, with a double-nudge guard

Every LinkedIn touch is still yours. Run search and Open profile only open tabs; Copy message only fills your clipboard. The workbench knows nothing you didn't click or type into it — by design.
